Luv or Pop

The dating app that wasn't supposed to be a dating app

Luv or pop is a dating app born out of a viral matchmaking YouTube game show called Pop the balloon. With more than 1.7 million subscribers on YouTube, and a Netflix show, it only felt fitting to expand the community into an app and help people find their connection.

Onboarding

(subscribe and verify first)

Problem

The app had 5 major entry friction gates and 2 of them were the biggest drop off points.

(experience first, hard parts later)

Solution

Shuffled the gates to increase user acquisition, subscription, verification and profile completion.

Details

The old onboarding hit users with a hard paywall after 4 or 5 screens, then gated profile creation and discovery behind document verification, so most users dropped off before seeing any value.

We had just started Mixpanel integration and I picked our master funnel to get the first insights. Steep drop off on 5 major points out of which 2 were crucial to go through.

When users download an app they carry some motivation, I used that momentum to make them finish their profile and explore the app a little before committing to higher friction tasks like subscription and verification. Users now had no gate on exploring potential profiles, engaging in community section, and watching exclusive content.

Approach

I studied the big 5 dating apps and researched on best onboarding processes. Turns out its context based. What works for a finance app might not work for a dating app. A spinning wheel for free credits works on a fun AI game but is misplaced on a social app.

I curated the onboarding to feel like human interaction. Naturally making users’ way up from email to basic details to preferences, using their hook for the show to reel them to new features, understanding their motivations and expectations and providing curated value.

I added small user questionnaires, creator messages, platform accolades, offers and multiple ways to explore the app to make it a dynamic experience.

Subscription

Problems

1.

Profiles were increasing but subscription was decreasing.

2.

80% of our userbase was on 7 day free trial, then churned.

3.

System level changes and new features needed support.

Solutions

Turned a static paywall, into a personalised subscription flow.

Curated with feedback forms, price anchoring, tiers, full disclosure, and contextual designs.

Introduced cancellation flow - pause subscription, coupon codes, referrals, one time offers and win backs.

Paywalls have no one best way, constant experimentation is key. I suggested tiers, price anchoring, transparency and copy tweaks, and made the flow humane and curated, like showing users their own profile mockup in the paywall. Offer campaigns followed, with Juneteenth giving a 65% rise. The PM's cancellation brief was to add friction, but I designed flows by cancellation reason instead: pause dating if tired, a referral code if they found someone, app features if unsure. No dark patterns, always a clean exit. Subscriptions rose quickly and the user momentum held.

From a dating app to an entertainment app

Why

We were selling intentional dating to an audience that came from a game show. We lacked direction, positioning and branding. On survey we found 60% of our users were show fans while we were building for the scattered 40%.

How

We doubled down on utilizing show aspects with new show formats for app, made community exciting with polls and quiz, and introduced games that give the same feel as the show - dating made fun.

Gamification came out of the flagship feature research. Our YouTube show was a fun game show around romance, so pitching a plain dating app to that audience was the wrong call.

I prototyped match based games, exciting ways to match in the show's spirit, which also added value to each find in our smaller pool. Community poll based matching briefly became V1 games but was killed for poor performance.

I also pitched Gems, a system level reward currency with 50 reward ideas, but it needed all rewards ready upfront so it was put for later. The match based games launched and built a daily user habit. My goal was to remove swiping fatigue, and with their success we began planning to remove swiping altogether.

Gamification

Problem

Nothing in the app rewarded anything. Searching, filtering, matching - none of it paid off, so there was no reason to go deeper, or come back once you drifted.

Users arrive with a clock in their head: no match, no renewal, gone. And despite 60% of our base being fans of the show, the app was a generic swipe product with nothing of the show in it.

Solution

A new game show but app native, and new match based games to double down on the show’s USP. Also Gems - a reward layer across the whole app that works as in app currency.

Every action earns, every tier unlocks something, and a visible year-long ladder is the map for users. And because users earn by engaging, every gem they chase is engagement we earn.

Result

0.0x

Increment in DAU under 4 weeks. These became our core app function!

Details

Dating apps last about 5 years and most, even Tinder, Bumble and Hinge, were past their peak. We were yet another one, with users from our YouTube show arriving every episode and leaving after the free trial. With engagement dipping, the team, CEO and CTO came together to brainstorm. My research showed a rise in offline dating events, coaches, mixers and blind dates, so I presented PRDs and wireframes for blind dating, speed dating, live Pop the Balloon and a mixer. After checking feasibility with dev, we picked blind dating, then Pop the Balloon. Both massively boosted our day 3 churn users and set off a chain reaction the next month: users, engagement, match rate, messages, shares and subscriptions all rose.
